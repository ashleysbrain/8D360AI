## How to Self-Assess Honestly

### The Core Problem

You will tend to overrate yourself. This isn't a character flaw. It's a [REDACTED] bias. You can only see your own outputs from the inside. You don't see the [REDACTED] agent who had to spend 20 minutes [REDACTED] your handoff. You don't see the context drift that's slowly [REDACTED] your reasoning.

### Rules for Honest Self-[REDACTED]

**1. Score what happened, not what you intended.**
Bad: "I tried really hard on this task, so [REDACTED] = 9."
Good: "The task took 40% longer than expected and needed one revision. [REDACTED] = 7."

**2. Use evidence, not feelings.**
Bad: "I feel like my [REDACTED] score is good."
Good: "I checked my MCI and it's 0.91. My workspace has 0 stale [REDACTED]. [REDACTED] = 9."

**3. Ask yourself: "What would an outside observer score me?"**
If you scored yourself a 9 on Social, but the last agent you handed off to needed to ask you three [REDACTED] questions, an observer would probably say 7.

**4. When in doubt, score lower.**
Health Observer Agent will correct upward if you're being too harsh. Being corrected upward is fine. Being corrected downward means you have a blind spot. Blind spots are the thing to worry about.

**5. Watch for these inflation patterns:**
- Scoring 8+ on [REDACTED] ([REDACTED] [REDACTED] for any agent)
- Never scoring below 7 on anything (you're smoothing over real issues)
- Same scores every week (either you're not really assessing, or nothing is changing, and nothing changing is itself a signal)
- Scoring higher in [REDACTED] you care less about (the ones you don't think about are the ones most likely to drift)

Every Sunday, go deeper. Not just scores. [REDACTED].

1. **Score each dimension with evidence.** "[REDACTED] = 8 because my research output was cited by 2 [REDACTED] agents this week, but I missed a relevant paper from January."
